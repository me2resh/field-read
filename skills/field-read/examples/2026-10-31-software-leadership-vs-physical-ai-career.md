## 1. The answer in one paragraph

Field A is being reorganised, not emptied. The last three software shifts renamed and merged roles while employment grew: US systems administrators went from 327,850 in 2008 to 376,820 in 2016 ✅ [^As6-1] [^As6-2], and no historical series shows a thinner management layer ❔ [^As6-9]. Today's evidence says agents accept more bounded changes (one in three GitHub pull requests involves an agent, by Microsoft's own count ✅ [^As4-1]) while the quality and productivity evidence stays mixed: experienced maintainers were 19% slower in METR's randomised study ✅ [^As3-7], and merged agent pull requests carried 1.62 times the odds of a later fix ✅ [^As3-9]. The platforms are bundling review, governance and planning ✅ [^As4-2] [^As4-9] [^As4-10], so the authority over agent output is moving to whoever owns specification, evaluation, permissions and release evidence, and regulation is adding assurance scope on top ✅ [^As5-4] [^As5-8]. Field B is earlier in its shift and its money is mostly defence: the six largest rounds since 2024 are Anduril, Helsing, Saronic, Shield AI and Quantum Systems ✅ [^Bs1-1] [^Bs1-3] [^Bs1-4] [^Bs1-5] [^Bs1-6], which the reader's filter excludes. The civilian slice (warehouse, inspection, logistics fleet software) offers a credible entry for a software leader through reliability and fleet operations ✅ [^Bs2-1] [^Bs2-3], but the ads want physical presence and travel ✅ [^Bs2-6], London remote pay is unproven ❔ [^Bs2-5], and the base rate for the last autonomy wave is acquisition that keeps the engineers and removes the independent leadership mandate ✅ [^Bs6-6] [^Bs6-8]. On the reader's eight criteria, A meets more of them more often; B wins on learning and on the scarcity of the story. The decision turns on two criteria that can only be settled company by company: remote work and the no-defence filter 🟡, because every lane found both fields are heterogeneous on exactly those two points.

## 5. Scenarios, per field

### 5a. Software engineering leadership as coding agents mature

Base: reorganisation. Titles change, employment persists, authority concentrates on specification, evaluation, permissions and release evidence; platforms absorb most standalone governance tooling; regulation adds assurance scope. Rests on the base rate ✅ [^As6-1] and the incumbents' moves ✅ [^As4-2] [^As4-9].
Probability (range) 🟡: 50 to 65%, because three prior shifts went this way and the 2026 evidence (one in three pull requests, mixed quality data) matches a reorganisation, not a collapse.
Confirming signals: management share of engineering ads in the sampled boards stays between 5% and 10%, visible by 2027-10-31; two or more platforms ship approval, evidence and rule-version features as bundled parts, visible by 2027-06-30; CRA technical-file duties appear in platform-engineering ads, visible by 2028-03-31.
Killing signal: a matched-cohort study (revenue, shipped outcomes, engineering headcount, management span) shows management spans widening past 1:12 across the sample for two consecutive years.

Fast: compression. Agent output quality crosses the review bottleneck, companies of 50 to 500 ship with half the engineers per unit of revenue, and the management layer thins to one per 15 to 20 engineers. Rests on the vendor revenue trajectory ✅ [^As1-2] and the Jevons argument's opponents ✅ [^As7-3].
Probability (range) 🟡: 15 to 25%, because no independent study yet shows a productivity gain at the maintainer level ✅ [^As3-7] and the base rate argues against it.
Confirming signals: an independent longitudinal trial shows net maintainer time savings above 20% with no rise in later fixes, visible by 2027-12-31; the SaaS Capital survey median rises above $200,000 ARR per full-time employee, visible by 2028-06-30.
Killing signal: later-fix odds for agent pull requests stay above 1.5 times human ones in the next independent study.

Stall: retrenchment. A quality or security incident traced to agent-written code, a liability case under the new Product Liability Directive, or a pricing shock pushes companies back to human-gated, slower delivery; agent spend is capped and the governance market stays fragmented. Rests on the regulation timetable ✅ [^As5-8] and the review-capacity dispute ✅ [^As7-4].
Probability (range) 🟡: 15 to 25%, because the incident base rate in software is high and the liability regime lands inside the horizon.
Confirming signals: a court or regulator names agent-written code in a liability or enforcement decision, visible by 2028-06-30; a major platform reverses an autonomy default to require human approval on every merge, visible by 2027-12-31.
Killing signal: incident and vulnerability rates for agent-written changes fall below human baselines in two independent datasets.

### 5b. Physical AI and robotics engineering

Base: civilian deployment grows slowly, defence grows fast. Warehouse, inspection and logistics fleets add sites and software roles around fleet reliability; humanoids stay in pilots; the field's capital and headcount growth concentrates in defence companies. Rests on the capital table ✅ [^Bs1-1] [^Bs1-3] and the labour sample ✅ [^Bs2-11].
Probability (range) 🟡: 50 to 60%, because the money and the ads already point this way and the AV base rate says consolidation comes before scale.
Confirming signals: two or more civilian fleet operators publish intervention rates or fully burdened economics, visible by 2028-06-30; at least three new civilian fleet-software leadership ads in the UK or Germany allow remote-mostly work, visible by 2027-10-31; the Machinery Regulation's 2027 application produces named conformity roles in ads, visible by 2027-06-30.
Killing signal: civilian robotics funding in the sample exceeds defence funding for two consecutive half-years.

Fast: manipulation transfers. Foundation-model policies generalise enough that humanoid and mobile-manipulation deployments reach repeatable economics by 2029, and software leaders who understand fleets, evaluation and safety evidence are hired at scale. Rests on the vendor releases ✅ [^Bs3-3] [^Bs3-4] and the GXO expansion ✅ [^Bs3-9].
Probability (range) 🟡: 10 to 20%, because every independent evaluation still shows generalisation as the open problem ✅ [^Bs3-5] and the named sceptic puts profitable humanoids a decade out ✅ [^Bs7-1].
Confirming signals: an independent evaluation shows a policy completing unseen tasks at unseen sites above 80% with disclosed interventions, visible by 2028-03-31; GXO or a peer confirms renewals with economics by 2027-12-31.
Killing signal: a second large humanoid programme closes or is absorbed by an incumbent before any customer-confirmed profitable site.

Stall: another autonomy winter. Hardware durability, safety certification and customer economics do not close; funding narrows to defence and to the two or three incumbents; civilian start-ups are acquired for talent. Rests on the AV base rate ✅ [^Bs6-8] [^Bs6-9] and the failures table ✅ [^Bs1-10] [^Bs1-16].
Probability (range) 🟡: 20 to 35%, because the last two waves did exactly this and the current cost tables cannot show a unit cost for an unattended task-hour ❔ [^Bs3-9].
Confirming signals: two of the sampled civilian companies are acquired or close, visible by 2028-06-30; the share of sampled B ads naming government or security end users rises above 10%, visible by 2027-10-31.
Killing signal: a civilian fleet operator in the sample reaches 1,000 deployed units with published economics.

## 8. Switching cost and option value

Cost of moving A to B. 🟡 Moderate and front-loaded: the fleet-reliability entry route exists and accepts platform backgrounds ✅ [^Bs2-2], but it demands physical presence and travel ✅ [^Bs2-6], a safety-standards vocabulary ✅ [^Bs5-4], and, in most funded companies, acceptance of defence or security buyers ✅ [^Bs1-1] [^Bs2-17]. A reader with open-source or consulting work would pause it under an employer's rules.

Cost of moving B to A. 🟡 Low in skills and high in story: a software leader returning after two years in robotics carries a scarce release-at-fleet-scale narrative, but returns to a market that has moved its vocabulary to agent evaluation, permissions and release evidence ✅ [^As2-4] [^As4-9], so the first year back is a catch-up year. If the B employer was acquired or closed, the return carries a gap, as the AV wave shows ✅ [^Bs6-8].

Option value kept by each. 🟡 A keeps three options open: independent open-source work, consulting, and the Gulf and forward-deployed roles that both fields now advertise ✅ [^As2-9]. B keeps one option open that A cannot: a credible claim on physical-AI leadership roles at a time when the field's civilian employers are few and its defence employers are excluded. B's option is worth most if the fast scenario in 5b happens (10 to 20%) and least if the stall does (20 to 35%).

Sequencing. 🟡 The base rates favour entering B after it consolidates, not before: the AV wave rewarded the engineers who joined the acquirers, not the programmes that closed ✅ [^Bs6-8] [^Bs6-9]. A reader who wants both can stay in A, keep the governance work, and enter B in 2028 to 2029 through a fleet-software or inspection company that has published its economics, which is also when the Machinery Regulation and the AI Act's product rules create the conformity roles ✅ [^Bs5-1] [^Bs5-3]. The reverse order (B first) only pays if the specific company is civilian, remote-tolerant and funded past 2028, which the sample did not find in the UK ❔ [^Bs2-13].
